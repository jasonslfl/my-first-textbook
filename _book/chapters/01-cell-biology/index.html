Cell Biology

Introduction

Every living organism, from a single-celled bacterium to a giant redwood tree, is made of cells. Cells carry out the processes that sustain life, including metabolism, growth, responses to the environment, and the transmission of genetic information.

Understanding cells helps explain how living organisms function, how they develop, and how life continues from one generation to the next.

Learning objectives

By the end of this section, you should be able to:

  • State the main principles of cell theory.
  • Explain why cells are considered the basic units of life.
  • Describe how evidence supports cell theory.
  • Explain why scientific theories can be revised when new evidence becomes available.

1.1 Cell theory

What is a cell?

A cell is the smallest unit of an organism capable of carrying out the processes associated with life. Some organisms consist of a single cell, while multicellular organisms contain many cells that may become specialized for different functions.

Despite their diversity, cells share several fundamental characteristics. They contain genetic material, carry out chemical reactions, and exchange materials and energy with their surroundings.

The principles of cell theory

Modern cell theory is based on three central principles:

  1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
  2. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
  3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

The first principle establishes that cells are the building blocks of living organisms. The second emphasizes that cells perform the processes necessary for life. The third explains that new cells form through the division of existing cells.

Together, these principles provide a foundation for understanding the organization and continuity of life.

Evidence and the development of cell theory

Cell theory developed through the work of many scientists and advances in microscopy. Observations of plant and animal tissues revealed that organisms consist of cells. Later observations of cell division helped establish that cells arise from existing cells.

This history illustrates an important feature of science: explanations are developed from observations and evidence, and scientific understanding can change as new methods and discoveries become available.

TipBiology tip

Distinguish between a cell and an organism. A bacterium can be a complete single-celled organism, whereas a muscle cell is one specialized component of a multicellular organism.
